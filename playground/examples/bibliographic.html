Knowledge Graphs in Publishing: A Survey

By A. Example — Institute for Linked Data Research

Abstract

This survey examines the application of knowledge graphs and linked data technologies in scholarly publishing. We review key standards, tooling, and adoption patterns across the publishing industry.

Introduction

Knowledge graphs have transformed information management in scholarly publishing [1]. The adoption of JSON-LD for semantic annotation has grown significantly in recent years [2], enabling richer machine-readable metadata alongside human-readable content.

Linked Data Foundations

Linked Data principles, first articulated by Berners-Lee [3], underpin the Semantic Web stack and are central to modern publishing workflows. The four rules — use URIs as names, use HTTP URIs, provide useful information, and include links to other URIs — remain the foundation of interoperable data on the web.
