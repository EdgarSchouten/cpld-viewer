Abstract

Element partitioning behavior between immiscible silicate melt and molybdate melt was determined by phase equilibrium experiments in the SiO2-B2O3-Al2O3-ZnO-CaO-Na2O-Li2O-MoO3 system at 1200ºC and 1000ºC (Figure 3.). By combining these results and thermodynamic data of silicate melts and molybdenum compounds, CALPHAD analysis was performed to construct a database for thermodynamic calculation of phase equilibria for MoO3-bearing alumino-borosilicate melts.  The thermodynamic database will be useful for predicting the phase separation of molybdate melt and crystallization of Powellite (CaMoO4) in vitrification process of high-level radioactive waste.

1. Introduction

High-level radioactive waste (HLW) is mixed with borosilicate glass, vitrified and then disposed of in a geological repository [1]-[2]. In this process, attention must be paid to the behavior of molybdenum, one of the fission products, because molybdenum-bearing silicate melt exhibit liquid-liquid phase separation [3]-[5].  When molybdate liquid formed by a phase separation is cooled, it crystallizes as water-soluble Na2MoO4 phase. Therefore, solubility and crystallization of molybdenum phase have been investigated in order to improve chemical durability of HLW glass [6]-[12]. In recent years, thermodynamic database for silicate systems has been developed by CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagrams) methodology [13]-[15]. The CALPHAD for a SiO2-Na2O-MoO3 system has also been carried out [4], [5], [16].

In this study, we performed the followings to predict the MoO3 solubility and phase separation of HLW glass melt by thermodynamic calculation.

(1) The chemical compositions of coexisting silicate and molybdate melts were investigated by phase equilibrium experiments in the SiO2-B2O3-Al2O3-ZnO-CaO-Na2O-Li2O-MoO3 (SBAZCNLM) system.

(2) CALPHAD calculation was performed in the B2O3-Na2O-Li2O-CaO-ZnO-MoO3 system.

(3) Combining the phase equilibrium data of (1), thermodynamic data of SiO2-Na2O-MoO3 system [5] and the interaction parameters by (2), CALPHAD calculation was carried out and thermodynamic database of the SBAZCNLM system was constructed. Some application examples are also shown in this paper.

2. Phase equilibrium experiments of MoO3-rich alumino-borosilicate system

Phase equilibrium experiments were carried out in the SBAZCNLM system. The experimental composition is described as

0.87(s[(1-x-y-p)SiO2-xB2O3-yAl2O3-pZnO]-(1-s)[(1-z-q)Na2O-zCaO-qLi2O])-0.13MoO3

where s is a mole fraction of total of network-former oxide and intermediate oxide (NF), x, y, and p are the proportions of B2O3, Al2O3, and ZnO in the NF, respectively, z and q are the proportions of CaO and Li2O in the network-modifier oxides (NM). In this study, 51 different compositions were prepared in the range of s=0.800-0.620, x=0.397-0.186, y=0.100-0.010, p=0.070-0.027, z=0.353-0.100, q=0.600-0.100.  In all starting composition, excess amount of MoO3 (13mol%) was added to achieve phase separation of silicate melt and molybdate melt.

The experimental method was basically the same as that used for other MoO3-bearing systems reported previously [5, 12]. High purity reagents of SiO2, B2O3, Al2O3, ZnO, Na2CO3, CaCO3, Li2CO3, and MoO3 were weighed, mixed in an agate mortar for 30 minutes, and then decarbonated at 550 ºC for 12 hours. Approximately 8.5 g of the decarbonated material was placed in a platinum crucible and held at 1200ºC or 1000ºC for 24 hours, and then quenched by water cooling of crucible with a rate of 20ºC/sec. Our preliminary experiments confirmed that equilibrium was reached within 24 hours.

The quenched sample separated into an upper silicate glass and a molybdenum-rich precipitate. Each sample was embedded in resin, polished, and observed by Electron-probe micro analyzer (JEOL-JXA8230). The silicate glass and molybdate precipitate was analyzed by EPMA for B2O3, Inductively Coupled Plasma Atomic Emission Spectroscopy (SII SPS-7700) for Li2O and X-ray Fluorescence (Rigaku ZSX Primus II) for other oxides.

The amount of silicate phase and molybdate phase was determined by mass-balance calculation based on the initial composition and analytical composition.  Volatilization loss at 1200ºC was estimated to be 1~10wt% for B2O3 and less than 0.5wt% for Na2O. The thermodynamic analysis was performed using the net bulk compositions by analysis, considering the losses of these elements.

Example of the chemical compositions of coexisting silicate melt and molybdate melt is shown in Table 1.  We observed that the solubility of MoO3 in silicate melts increased and amount of molybdate melt decreased with increasing bulk B2O3/(SiO2+Al2O3), CaO/Na2O and temperature.

Liquid-liquid phase separation occurs when two local minima are formed in the Gibbs energy of liquid phase due to the competition between the negative - T S Mix term and the positive H Mix . The phase separation of borosilicate melt and molybdate melt is due to the fact that Mo6+ ions with a large coordination number have difficulty entering the network structure of silicate melt.

3-2. Results

Thermodynamic calculations were able to largely reproduce the element partitioning by phase separation observed in experiments, but there was a tendency to slightly overestimate Na2O in silicate melt and CaO and Li2O in molybdate melt. These discrepancies may be seemingly resolved by considering the temperature dependence of the parameters or higher order parameters, but in the absence of thermochemical evidence to support them, they were not considered further in this study.

4. Application of thermodynamic database

Using a thermodynamic database, we performed several calculations on the dissolution behavior of MoO3 in Japanese simulated HLW glasses. Figure 3. shows the composition dependence of the X MoO 3 SL at 1200ºC. The MoO3 solubility of the current glass bead was calculated to be 10.9wt%. Thermodynamic calculation shows that when the amount of Na2O+Li2O+CaO+ZnO is less than 20mol%, the X MoO 3 SL increases significantly with increasing (B2O3 - Al2O3)/SiO2.

Figure 3. shows the liquidus phase diagram for the current glass bead composition and its surroundings. It is predicted that phase separation becomes more likely to occur as the Na2O/CaO ratio decreases and the SiO2/B2O3 ratio increases and is reduced with decreasing MoO3 content.

5. Summary

Phase equilibrium experiments were performed in the SiO2-B2O3-Al2O3-ZnO-CaO-Na2O-Li2O-MoO3 system to clarify the compositional relationship between coexisting silicate melt and molybdate melt. Using experimental data and thermodynamic data on borosilicate melts, the thermodynamic database to calculate dissolution behavior of MoO3 was constructed through CALPHAD analysis.  In high-level waste glass compositions, the higher the CaO/Na2O ratio and the B2O3/(SiO2+Al2O3) ratio, the higher the MoO3 solubility and suppress phase separation. This behavior can be reproduced by using thermodynamic database.
